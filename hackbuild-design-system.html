01 The rules

This is the punk zine language: warm paper, black ink, one hot pink, typewriter type, hard shadows that look slightly off-register like a bad photocopy. It is the identity for hack.build itself and for every bench tool built under it. It is not LumenCanvas (paper and teal, Sora) and not CLASP (charcoal, JetBrains Mono). Those are separate systems and they never mix in one artifact.

Always

  • Paper background, ink text, pink as the single accent
  • Four faces with four jobs, never one face for everything
  • Hard offset shadows, zero blur
  • Zero border radius, except the section number and nav dots
  • Font Awesome or inline SVG icons
  • The canonical two-path mark, placed through its calibrated viewBox

Never

  • Emoji, anywhere, including in code comments and UI strings
  • Em dashes or en dashes, in prose or in generated output
  • Background gradients, blurred shadows, rounded corners
  • A second accent color competing with the pink
  • A redrawn, traced, or cleaned-up version of the mark
  • The old #ff3366 pink from the explainer skill
Color correction of record. Several older skill files and templates carry #ff3366, #ff2d7b, or #f5f2eb. Those are wrong. The favicon values win: pink #FE0386, paper #f5f0e6, ink #1a1a1a. If a file disagrees with this page, this page is right.

02 The mark

Two paths taken verbatim from the site favicon: a broken arc in ink and a wrench in pink. Never approximate it, never redraw it, never trace it from a render. Place it through the calibrated square viewBox 117 103 800 800; the arc sits at translate(490,121) and the wrench at translate(498,314). A guessed transform produced a wrong-sized mark once and it was rejected.

Wordmark

hack.build

IBM Plex Mono at weight 700, matching the live site. hack in ink, then the dot and build both in pink. Setting only the dot in pink is the most common mistake. On an ink header, hack flips to paper and .build stays pink.

Sizing

  • Mark alone: 16 px floor on screen, 8 mm floor in print
  • Horizontal lockup: 120 px wide floor, below that drop to the mark
  • Stacked lockup with tagline: 2 inch floor, the tagline dies below it
  • On a PCB, the wrench becomes a mask opening in bare gold and the arc becomes silk

Misuse

  • Do not recolor the wrench to anything but the pink
  • Do not add a stroke, glow, bevel, or drop shadow to the mark
  • Do not rotate, skew, or stretch it non-uniformly
  • Do not put it on a busy photo without a solid paper or ink plate behind it
  • Do not rebuild the wordmark in Permanent Marker

03 Color

One accent. The pink is a punctuation mark, not a paint. It marks the single most important thing in a view: the active tab, the live value, the primary action, the trace on the scope. If three things on screen are pink, none of them are.

Where the pink goes

  • Active tab underline and header bottom rule
  • Live readouts, the plotted trace, the beam dot halo
  • Focus rings and the pressed state of the primary button
  • The dot and build in the wordmark

Where it does not

  • Large fills or page backgrounds
  • Body copy at paragraph length
  • More than one competing element per view
  • Chrome that is only decorative

Signal colors

For data only. Charts, log severity, pass and fail. Never chrome, never brand. These are tuned for text on paper.

The lit set

The paper signal colors go muddy on the void. Anything drawn inside a stage, a scope or a console uses the lit set instead. Every tool arrived at this split independently before it was written down, which is why it is a rule now rather than a preference.

Two panel heads

Permanent Marker on a rule is the editorial head: pages, docs, marketing, and the calmer half of a tool. VT323 on an ink bar is the instrument head: dense panels, anything sitting next to a scope. Pick one per artifact and use it for every panel in that artifact. Mixing them inside one view is the fastest way to make a tool look unfinished.

05 Structure

Shadows

Hard offset, no blur, no spread. They read as a print registration error, which is the whole point. Pressing a control collapses its shadow and moves it by the same offset, so the thing physically goes down.

3px, controls
5px, panels and cards
7px, hover and hero
5px pink, the one stage

Borders and radius

2 px ink on panels, cards, fields and buttons. 3 px for the header rule, the active tab underline, and focus rings. Dashed 2 px for internal dividers. Radius is zero on everything; the only exceptions are the circular section number and nav dots. A rounded button means the artifact has left the system.

Texture

Covered in full in the next section, because which texture you pick follows from which surface you are on.

06 Surfaces and texture

There are five surfaces and they form a ladder. Going up the ladder means adding a border and a shadow, not adding a color. Going to the void means leaving paper entirely, and the void is reserved: it is where the machine draws, never where you put chrome.

Which texture, and when

Pick exactly one per artifact. Apply it once, at the page level, as a fixed overlay or a body background. Layering two turns the paper to mud, and applying one per component makes the seams visible where elements overlap.

halftone dots

Use for dense tool UIs and app chrome, where grain would fight with small type and thin rules. 4 px radial grid at 4.5 percent. This is what KERF, DETENT and the board viewers run.

grain

Use for pages, zines, stickers, decks and anything that should feel printed. Fractal noise, 0.05 on paper and 0.07 over ink, as one fixed full-viewport overlay above everything. This page runs grain.

graph rule

Only inside a stage, behind a plot. 32 px pink grid at 10 percent on the void. It is a graticule, not a decoration, so it never appears on paper.

Rules that get broken. Texture never goes on a stage, a canvas, a dial plate, or anything that will be screenshotted for a datasheet. It is disabled in print. It is disabled inside a modal. And it never scrolls: a grain layer that moves with the content reads as a rendering bug rather than paper.

Code

/* halftone, on the body */
background-image: radial-gradient(rgba(26,26,26,.045) 1px, transparent 1px);
background-size: 4px 4px;

/* grain, one fixed overlay, never per element */
body::before{
  content:""; position:fixed; inset:0; z-index:9999;
  pointer-events:none; opacity:.05;
  background-image:url("data:image/svg+xml,...feTurbulence fractalNoise 0.85...");
}

/* graph rule, inside a stage only */
background-image:
  linear-gradient(rgba(254,3,134,.10) 1px, transparent 1px),
  linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(254,3,134,.10) 1px, transparent 1px);
background-size: 32px 32px;

Classes: .hb-halftone, the body.hb::before grain, .hb-graph.

Header anatomy

Left to right, always in this order: the mark at 26 to 28 px, the tool name in Permanent Marker with the trailing dot or suffix in pink, the connection status pill, then everything else pushed right with margin-left auto, and the tagline last at reduced opacity. The mark appears here and nowhere else in the app. The title is the tool name, not hack.build; the mark is what carries the brand.

On a marketing page instead

A page header inverts: paper rather than ink, taller, the horizontal lockup rather than the bare mark, no status, no tabs. It gets one pink element, usually the call to action. If the page scrolls, the header shrinks to the ink tool-style bar rather than staying tall.

Do

  • One status pill in the header, showing the connection, not the task
  • Mode switches in the header, verbs in the toolbar
  • Tabs directly under the header with no gap
  • Push secondary things right with .hb-spacer
  • Pin the header, tabs and status strip; scroll only the work area

Do not

  • Put connect, save or export in the header
  • Put the mark in the toolbar or the footer as well
  • Stack more than four strips before content starts
  • Give the status strip a button
  • Let the tab row wrap to two lines; collapse to a select first

The pieces a bench tool needs that a page does not. One dial per tool, one sticker per view, one meter per signal.

VT323 on an ink plate, pink digits, tabular so it stops jittering. Digits are individually clickable: top half raises, bottom half lowers. The sticker is the one place Permanent Marker is allowed at small size.

09 Patterns

Tool chrome

The recipe every bench tool follows, proven across the laser rigs, the radios, and the board viewers.

  1. Paper body with the halftone dot field, fixed grain overlay optional.
  2. Ink header bar, 3 px pink bottom rule, the mark at about 26 px, Permanent Marker title with the pink span, Special Elite tagline pushed right at reduced opacity.
  3. Nav tabs directly under the header, transparent on ink, pink underline on the active one.
  4. Content as panels on raised paper with 2 px ink borders and 5 px block shadows.
  5. Any canvas, scope, waterfall or 3D view is a stage: the void, a pink offset shadow, cream text inside.
  6. A console pinned at the bottom of the working column, newest line first.
  7. Every control is Plex Mono uppercase. Every number is VT323 pink.

Page and zine

Sections alternate paper and ink so the ink bands act as gaps. Hero gets the big Permanent Marker headline, often with a solid ink block sitting behind one word. Registration marks in the corners, diagonal pink rules, scattered background phrases at low opacity, and a large faded numeral behind each section. Body text stays under 68 characters per line and never runs more than three paragraphs before something interactive or visual.

Sticker and seal

Circular seals put band text curving the top edge in Special Elite caps, the tagline along the bottom in Libre Baskerville italic, pink bursts at 9 and 3 o'clock, the mark centered, and the wordmark under it. The cut line is the outermost disc and the only cut line. Every design must be a contained single shape with nothing floating, so it weeds clean. Set all type as outlined paths, not live text, so the file has zero font dependency at the printer.

On a board

Pink soldermask, white silk, gold exposed copper. The wrench becomes a mask opening so it comes out in bare metal and the arc stays silk. When pink mask is not available, invert: cream or white mask, black silk, gold. The mark must sit inside a counter-flipped wrapper on a bottom-side render or it prints mirrored.

10 Voice and icons

Write like this

  • Lowercase UI copy, terse, no ceremony
  • Plain statements of what a control does and what it costs
  • Name the failure mode instead of hiding it
  • Second person for instructions: connect the board, then adopt its config
  • Short paragraphs, then show the thing working

Not like this

  • Em dashes and en dashes, anywhere
  • Emoji, including in log output
  • Marketing adjectives, exclamation marks, hype
  • Bold and italic scattered through prose
  • Filler openers like it is worth noting or in today's world

Icons are Font Awesome 6 solid or hand-written inline SVG at 1.5 to 2.4 stroke width, currentColor, no fills. Never emoji, and never an icon where a two-word label is clearer.
